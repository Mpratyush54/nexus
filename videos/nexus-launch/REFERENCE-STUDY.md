# Reference study — 24 October 2026

Primary sources, not third-party “Apple-style” templates:

- Apple, MacBook Pro product film, linked from https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/
- Apple, iPhone Air product film, linked from https://www.apple.com/iphone-air/
- Apple UK, *Introducing the all-new Mac mini*:
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bXNYCpZdXAE

The first two official Apple film streams were decoded to private contact
sheets at three-second intervals over their first 72 seconds. Sheets live in
ignored `dist/reference-study/`, not delivery assets. No Apple footage, music,
typeface, copy or logo is included in the Nexus film. The YouTube reference
was located and its official description read; it was not visually sampled.

## Observed, not claimed as an Apple-wide formula

MacBook Pro: repeated changes between hands/keyboard details, a recognisable
whole laptop and people doing work. Copy is integrated with that action. It
is not six left-aligned headlines with a small interface below each one.

iPhone Air: the thin edge is established before its surrounding world is
revealed. The same phone and movement connect detail, hand and wider room.
Scale and framing change dramatically; the product remains recognisable.
Light and dark environments establish different acts.

Sampling at three seconds is not a cut detector. Exact shot lengths, motion
curves and musical accents were not measured. Sound was not auditioned.

## Nexus translation — a design inference

- Product first: one recognisable conversation, not an opening title slide.
- Macro → whole workspace → macro. Show one control/result at a time.
- Carry the same reply/patch between agents, sharing and retrieval. The next
  view must be caused by an action, not introduced by another slogan.
- Eighteen authored shot/action cues in sixty seconds; readable demonstration
  holds alternate with 0.35–0.65 second camera moves. This is our timing,
  not a claimed measurement of Apple’s edit.
- Use the real Nexus palette and components. Do not imitate hardware renders
  by tilting software windows in fake 3D or adding lens flares.
- Sparse editorial copy, strong light/dark acts, one restrained end lockup.

The rejected draft is retained in HyperFrames history. Its passing technical
checks did not establish that it matched the requested creative direction.
